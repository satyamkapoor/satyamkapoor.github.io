This summer, we decided to embark on a long distance train journey. You'd ask why? Especially when train tickets are supposedly more expensive, reliability is questionable, and when Europe is getting hit with scorching high temperatures. I still do not have a clear answer, it just felt more free - we could get off and board at any stop, in any country. Also, a car isn't an option with a toddler who hates to sit calmly in it for more than 20 minutes.

### Cost of rail Freedom - an Interrail pass

Train travel across Europe is notoriously expensive. Especially in countries where it works with dynamic pricing (unlike Belgium). An Interrail pass, for those who aren't aware, allows you to board any train in Europe without buying a ticket. The pass is decently priced - 7 days in a month costs [$381](https://www.interrail.com/en/interrail-passes/global-pass) for an adult. Looks exciting, easy and liberating, right?

#### You can't put a price on Freedom

Happy, relaxed and naive, we thought everything was going to be so easy, after all we had the Freedom - an Interrail pass.

So while we were saying bye to one of our hotels, after the wonderfully boring breakfast, and heading to the station to take our next leg, we discovered that all seats on the train were booked. Not only on this train, but on the next three out of the four daily trains on our route.

Some trains force you to reserve a seat before you board; sadly, the seat reservation is something which you have to do yourself and isn't included in the pass price. Let's put the costs apart - what was even more shocking, on certain routes, there aren't any seats available to be reserved for Interrail pass holders, as apparently, there is a quota for Interrail passes on trains.

If I have to reserve seats in advance for my travel, then I could very well purchase the ticket in advance too, so why would I need an Interrail pass? :/

#### Freedomless Vulnerable pass

Ok ok, we managed to get some seats reserved. Of course, you can't reserve them on the Interrail mobile app, but only on their web portal, where it forces you to create a trip again and add your pass again. The next big shocker was that despite Interrail suffering a [big data breach](https://cybernews.com/security/300000-travelers-eurail-data-breach/) just months ago, there is no option to add MFA to your account. So, whatever personal data I'm adding is probably going to end up in unauthorised hands again, and might be used to target me in future for phishing.

So the cost is not just for an Interrail pass, but to ensure that your private data reaches bad actors.
